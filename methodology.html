Every number traces to a named method.

This page exists for the person whose job is to be skeptical: a biostatistician, an AMS committee chair, a TMDA or MOH reviewer. Below is the exact arithmetic behind the figures the tools produce — written from the tools' source code, not from a marketing description of it. Where a method is a simplification, it says so.

Wilson score interval (proportions & prevalence)

Used for every proportion that is reported with a confidence interval. Replaces the common but statistically weaker practice of reporting a bare percentage.

z = 1.96 // 95% confidence p̂ = x / n d = 1 + z²/n center = (p̂ + z²/2n) / d half-width = z · √(p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/4n²) / d 95% CI = [center − half-width, center + half-width], clamped to [0, 1]

This is the textbook Wilson score interval — it behaves correctly at small sample sizes and near 0%/100%, where the more common normal-approximation (Wald) interval breaks down. PPS Pro marks any estimate whose denominator is below 20 with ⚠ rather than letting a wide interval pass unnoticed.

Applies in: PPS Pro (all 14 survey indicators, ward and statistics tables) · VigiWard (sequence-symmetry proportions; signal rates per 1,000 patients exposed in the facility report)

AWaRe classification — WHO, with a Tanzania view

Every antibiotic dispensed or prescribed is tagged Access, Watch or Reserve — WHO's framework for stewardship targets. Fixed-dose combinations that WHO lists as “Not recommended” are kept as their own group, never folded into one ingredient's class.

AMS Pro reports the WHO classification by default and shows a Tanzania STG/NEMLIT view side by side, so the effect of the ruler is visible. The Tanzania view changes only the agents listed below; the list is editable on the Setup page, because the pharmacist holding the current NEMLIT copy is the final authority — entries marked “check” should be confirmed against it.

A report run under WHO rules and one run under Tanzania rules can show different Access/Watch/Reserve splits for the same prescribing — not because prescribing changed, but because the ruler changed. Every AWaRe figure states which rule set produced it. PPS Pro and VigiWard use the WHO classification.

Target used: at least 70% of human antibiotic use from the Access group by 2030 (UN General Assembly Political Declaration on AMR, 2024), which supersedes the earlier WHO target of 60%. AWaRe shares in AMS Pro are shares of antibiotic dispensing lines; a DDD-weighted share is shown beside them.

DDD and DOT per 1,000 patient-days

Antimicrobial consumption expressed in WHO ATC/DDD units — the standard way to compare consumption across periods or facilities regardless of ward size.

// for each antibiotic dispensing line (computed per line, then summed) grams = quantity × strength read from the item name (e.g. "Ceftriaxone … 1g Vial" → 1 g) DDD = grams ÷ WHO DDD for that agent and route (parenteral or oral) DOT = planned days of therapy read from the dosage text ("Duration: 5 /7" → 5) DDD per 1,000 patient-days = Σ DDD ÷ patient-days × 1,000 DOT per 1,000 patient-days = Σ DOT ÷ patient-days × 1,000

The route is taken from the item's form (injection, infusion, vial → parenteral; tablet, capsule, suspension → oral) or, failing that, from the dosage text. For amoxicillin-clavulanate the WHO DDD refers to the amoxicillin content, so labelled totals (625 mg, 1.2 g …) are converted to their amoxicillin content. A line gets no DDD — rather than a guessed one — when the strength is missing from the item name, when it is an oral liquid whose bottle volume is unknown, or when WHO assigns no DDD to the combination; the tool reports the share of lines covered. A unit strength can be supplied on the Dose Reference page, and lines computed that way are counted as “assumed”.

Patient-days are measured when an admissions file with bed-days or admission dates is imported (always preferred), or estimated from beds × occupancy × days entered on Setup — and the label on every figure says which. Without either, totals are still shown, but no per-1,000 rate is computed.

PPS Pro reports a survey-day figure instead: Σ(dose × doses per day ÷ WHO DDD) for all patients on the survey day, per 100 inpatients surveyed.

No “acceptable range”: WHO publishes no target value for hospital DDD or DOT rates. Compare against your own baseline over time, and against published facilities of similar type, with the same denominator. Applies in: AMS Pro (Dashboard, Consumption, Analytics) · PPS Pro (DDD per 100 inpatients)

Run charts with control limits (Laney p′)

Answers the committee's real question — “did something change, or is this ordinary variation?” — for weekly or monthly shares.

// each point i = one week or month; x_i of n_i antibiotic lines meet the criterion p̄ = Σx_i / Σn_i // centre line σ_i = √( p̄(1−p̄) / n_i ) z_i = (p_i − p̄) / σ_i σ_z = max( 1, mean|z_i − z_i−1| / 1.128 ) // Laney's between-point correction limits_i = p̄ ± 3 · σ_i · σ_z, clamped to [0, 1]

With hundreds of lines per point, classic p-chart limits are so narrow that ordinary week-to-week differences between patients and prescribers look like alarms; Laney's correction widens them by the variation actually observed between points. Partial first and last weeks are dropped. A point is flagged when it falls outside the limits, when 8 or more consecutive points sit on one side of the centre line (a shift), or when 6 consecutive points rise or fall (a trend). About 20 points are recommended before trusting the limits, and a volume/department-mix chart is shown alongside so a change in who was captured is not mistaken for a change in prescribing.

Applies in: AMS Pro Run Charts — Access share, Watch share, WHO Not-recommended share, injectable share, and the share of antibiotic patients with a time-out flag

Trends over time

Two settings, two methods: many monthly points from dispensing data, and a handful of survey rounds from PPS.

Monthly series (AMS Pro Resistance Trend) — ordinary least squares

x = month index 0, 1, 2 … y = monthly Watch+Reserve share (or other chosen measure) slope = Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ) / Σ(x−x̄)² R² = goodness of fit change per year = slope × 12 ÷ mean(y) × 100% 95% band = slope ± t · SE(slope), with t ≈ 1.96 (n > 30), 2.16 (n 10–30), 2.45 (n < 10)

At least 3 months are required. The band is built from the uncertainty of the slope only, and the t-values are a rounded approximation — adequate for “is this falling or not”, not for a publication-grade interval. The line is a proxy for resistance-driving prescribing pressure, not a measured resistance rate (that comes from the antibiogram).

Survey rounds (PPS Pro) — tests for proportions

// Cochran–Armitage test for trend; scores s_i = time of survey i in years p̄ = Σx_i / Σn_i T = Σ s_i (x_i − n_i p̄) Var(T) = p̄(1−p̄) [ Σ n_i s_i² − (Σ n_i s_i)² / Σ n_i ] Z = T / √Var(T), two-sided p from the normal distribution

Alongside it: a chi-square test across all rounds (any difference, no direction assumed); first-versus-latest difference in percentage points with a 95% Wald interval and a pooled two-proportion z-test; and a straight-line change per year in percentage points (descriptive only, as a few rounds give little power for a slope).

Applies in: AMS Pro Resistance Trend · PPS Pro Trends and Statistics pages, Overview key messages and the trend report

Projections and scenarios — deliberately simple

Where a chart continues a line into the future, it is a directional continuation of the recent past, labelled as such — never a forecast.

// AMS Pro Analytics (monthly DDDs) and Cost & Impact (monthly spend, shown only when ticked) recent change = average of the last 3 month-on-month changes // % for DDDs, shillings for spend projection = last month carried forward by that change, month by month // Stewardship scenario outlook (user-adjustable assumptions) without AMS = today × (1 + drift%)^years current trajectory = today × (1 + r)^years, r = (1 + recent monthly change)^12 − 1, held between the improvement and drift rates, not below the floor full stewardship = today × (1 − improvement%)^years, not below the floor

These are arithmetic what-ifs that make an assumption visible and adjustable — useful for a budget conversation, not evidence of what will happen.

Applies in: AMS Pro Analytics (monthly trend + projection, scenario outlook) and Cost & Impact (optional projection)

Cumulative antibiogram

Built from imported culture & sensitivity results, following the CLSI M39 conventions for cumulative antibiograms.

first isolate per patient per organism only (earliest by sample date) %S = S / (S + I + R) // intermediate counts against susceptibility cells with fewer than 30 isolates are shown with ⚠, not hidden empirical therapy review: an antibiotic in use is flagged when its overall %S across tested organisms is below 80%

The table can be filtered by period and specimen. The empirical review is deliberately crude (overall coverage, not indication-specific) and says so on screen.

Applies in: AMS Pro Antibiogram

Sequence symmetry analysis (prescribing cascades)

Used for flagging potential adverse-drug-reaction signals and prescribing-cascade patterns from pharmacy dispensing sequences alone, without a causality-coded dataset.

For each drug pair (A, B), the tool counts patients whose first A preceded their first B versus the reverse, applies a Wilson confidence interval to the forward/total proportion and transforms it into a ratio interval via p / (1−p) — valid because the transform is monotonic.

A signal is only flagged when the lower confidence bound clears a ratio of 1 and there are at least 5 discordant pairs — a deliberate minimum-power guard against false positives on sparse data. Each ratio is reported both crude and adjusted for prescribing trends (divided by the null-effect sequence ratio expected from how the two drugs' use changed over the period), and every flagged signal is labeled hypothesis-generating, not proof of causation — it tells a pharmacist where to look, not what the answer is.

Applies in: VigiWard Overview (sequence symmetry) alongside its patient-level cascade signals

Spike alerts (possible product-quality problems)

A sudden rise in reactions to one medicine in one month or week can point to a bad batch, a new supplier or a substandard or falsified product rather than to the patients.

// for each medicine × reaction series, and each period p observed O_p = patients with that reaction signal in period p expected λ_p = Σ_wards [ patients exposed in that ward in p × reaction rate in that ward in all OTHER periods ] p-value = P( X ≥ O_p | X ~ Poisson(λ_p) ) // exact upper tail alert when O_p ≥ 3, O_p / λ_p ≥ 2 and p < 0.01

Each period is compared with all other periods (leave-one-out), with the department mix adjusted for (indirect standardisation), so a month in which more high-risk wards were captured does not pass for a spike. Series with fewer than 5 events or 30 exposed patients are not tested; dismissed signals and premedication patterns are excluded. Because many series and periods are tested, about 1 in 100 tests can reach p < 0.01 by chance — the page states how many such chance alerts to expect, and every alert is a reason to check batch numbers and the supplier, not a finding.

Applies in: VigiWard Spike alerts

IPC indicators (Tanzania MOH GHS-CQI)

Infection prevention and control indicators follow the Tanzania Ministry of Health Global Health Security continuous quality improvement (GHS-CQI) framework, with the Tanzania National IPC Guidelines and WHO/AFRO IPC core components as references.

AMS Pro carries twelve IPC indicators: surgical site infection, hazardous waste segregation, quarterly IPC programme assessment, IPCAF score, hand hygiene compliance (WHO “My 5 Moments”), CAUTI, VAP and CLABSI rates per 1,000 device-days, sharps injuries, WASH availability, standard precautions and CSSD reprocessing. The SSI rate after caesarean section is computed automatically from the admissions data (confirmed SSI ÷ caesarean sections, also shown on CRF02); the others are observation- or audit-based and entered on the Indicators page with their numerator and denominator.

Applies in: AMS Pro Indicators and CRF Forms; exported as the GHS-CQI indicator CSV for facility submission
