PPS Pro

A WHO-methodology point prevalence survey tool: capture data ward by ward, get automatic AWaRe classification and a properly-computed confidence interval on every prevalence figure — not a raw percentage dressed up as one.

Download PPS Pro See the Wilson CI math

How a survey runs

1. Patient capture

Enter each surveyed patient directly — ward, gender, whether they're on an antibiotic that day, indication, and antibiotic details. Built for entering data at the bedside or straight after the ward round, not for retyping a paper form back at a desk.

2. Multi-surveyor merge

When more than one person surveys different wards on the same day, each surveyor's file merges into one dataset — with duplicate patient-code detection flagged for manual review rather than silently overwritten.

3. Data quality pass

Before analysis, the tool checks integrity: duplicate patient codes, missing indication links, incomplete antibiotic-to-indication mapping — the same checks a statistician would run by hand, run automatically.

4. Executive snapshot

Overall antimicrobial prevalence with a Wilson 95% confidence interval, AWaRe mix (Access/Watch/Reserve), antibiotics per treated patient, and a ward-by-ward breakdown — ready to present to the AMS committee or export as a report.

Every figure above was reproduced independently from the raw survey export, including a from-scratch Wilson score interval calculation — not taken from the tool's own summary on faith. The raw export also contained the ordinary mess of real fieldwork: the same ward recorded under five different spellings (WD-B4, WDB4, a stray space, a duplicated letter). PPS Pro's data-quality pass exists precisely because that kind of mess is the norm, not the exception.
