PPS Pro

A WHO-methodology point prevalence survey tool: capture data ward by ward, get automatic AWaRe classification and a properly-computed confidence interval on every figure — then follow every indicator across survey rounds with formal trend tests, so you can show whether stewardship is working.

Open the live demo Download PPS Pro

How a survey runs

1. Patient capture

Enter each surveyed patient directly — ward, age, sex, weight for children, whether they're on an antimicrobial that day, diagnosis, indication and up to four agents with dose and route — or import a CSV per survey. ATC code and AWaRe group are filled in automatically, and common brand names are recognised.

2. Multi-surveyor merge

When more than one person surveys different wards on the same day, each surveyor's file merges into the same survey — and patient codes that appear in both files are listed on the data-quality page for review, never silently overwritten.

3. Data quality pass

Before analysis, the tool checks integrity: duplicate patient codes, missing ward or age, admission after the survey date, hospital-acquired infection within 48 hours of admission, prophylaxis without surgery, missing doses or routes — the same checks a statistician would run by hand, run automatically.

4. Trends across rounds

Every indicator is followed across survey rounds: prevalence, Access/Watch/Reserve share, parenteral route, reason in notes, guideline compliance, culture, stop/review date, surgical prophylaxis beyond one day and more — each with a Cochran–Armitage test for trend, ward heat-maps and a printable trend report.

5. Executive snapshot

Overall antimicrobial prevalence with a Wilson 95% confidence interval, AWaRe mix (Access/Watch/Reserve), antibiotics per treated patient, and a ward-by-ward breakdown — ready to present to the AMS committee or export as a report.

Every figure above was reproduced independently from the raw survey export, including a from-scratch Wilson score interval calculation — not taken from the tool's own summary on faith. The raw export also contained the ordinary mess of real fieldwork: the same ward recorded under five different spellings (WD-B4, WDB4, a stray space, a duplicated letter). PPS Pro's data-quality pass exists precisely because that kind of mess is the norm, not the exception.
