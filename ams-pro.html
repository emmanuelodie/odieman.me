AMS Pro

Turn the exports your hospital system already produces — pharmacy dispensing, admissions, laboratory and culture results — into a working antimicrobial stewardship programme: AWaRe and consumption analysis, an antibiogram, statistically sound trend charts, patient-level review lists, CRFs, and reports ready for the AMS committee.

Open the live demo Request AMS Pro

What it takes in, what it gives back

Input

A pharmacy dispensing export (item name, quantity, date — plus dosage, ward, price and prescriber where available), optionally with the admissions register, laboratory results with turnaround times, culture & sensitivity results (or a WHONET isolate listing), a procedures or theatre report and the store’s ward issue report. Several files per type are merged automatically, so three yearly exports become one three-year analysis.

Output

AWaRe shares under WHO and Tanzania rules, DDD and DOT per 1,000 patient-days, monthly trends, run charts, an antibiogram with a printable A4 page, daily review lists, cost analysis, indicators and CRFs — and a Deep Report as an editable Word document and PowerPoint deck. For an improvement project it adds before–after tests and interrupted time series, and for ward rounds a printable form and a monthly round report.

The modules

32 pages in four groups, each explained inside the tool's built-in user guide.

Clinical records

Patients · Prescriptions · Antibiogram — one line per patient joined across pharmacy, lab, admissions and procedures; every dispensing line with its matched generic, AWaRe class, route, days and DDD; and a cumulative antibiogram counting the first isolate per patient per organism (CLSI M39), with cells under 30 isolates flagged, and printed on one A4 page for the wards.

AMS operations

Dashboard · C/S Prophylaxis Project · AMS Ward Rounds · Antibiotic Time-out · Bug–drug Check · Consumption · Cost & Impact · Analytics · Run Charts · Did It Work? · Intervention Impact · Driver Zones · Resistance Trend · What's Working · Discrepancy · PDCA — a caesarean-section prophylaxis project from baseline to end-term report, ward round forms and their monthly results, the daily review list (IV ≥3 days, double anaerobic cover, long courses, Reserve without culture, prophylaxis beyond 24 h), mismatches between culture results and the antibiotic given, Laney p′ control charts, trend lines with confidence bands, spend by AWaRe tier, and recognition for wards and prescribers doing well.

Reporting & indicators

Reports · Indicators · WHO/INRUD · CRF Forms · Export — the Deep Report in Word and PowerPoint, Tanzania GHS-CQI AMS and IPC indicators, WHO/INRUD prescribing indicators, CRF01 dispensing, CRF02 SSI after caesarean section and CRF03 audit log.

Setup & data

User Guide · Minimum Data · Decision brief · Setup · Dose Reference · Data Entry · Manual entry (any form) · Import Hub — what each analysis needs and a check of the data loaded, a one-page brief for the committee, facility details, the AWaRe scheme, an editable WHO ATC/DDD dose reference, manual entry on any form, and an import hub that recognises messy real-world exports (title rows, repeated headers, missing columns) and can undo any single import.

Saving

Data folder — choose a folder once and everything is saved there automatically: the database, a daily backup, a safety copy before any reset or restore, and every export and report. Nothing in the folder is overwritten without being read first.

Showcase (5.7)

A stage for a talk (★ Showcase in the top bar, in the live demo too). AMS Pro opens empty; the sample hospital's exports go in one by one, pharmacy, admissions, laboratory, cultures, procedures and stock issues, the counts rise on an assembly line, and a ledger shows that rows in = rows used + rows set aside. Each headline figure starts red, naming the file it still needs, and turns green when that file arrives. Then Reports, one click writes real files from the data just loaded: the full report in Word, the committee slides in PowerPoint, the printable antibiogram, the WHONET isolate file, the WHO GLASS summary, the WHO ATC/DDD consumption table, the REDCap data dictionary and the Stata data with its do-file. It ends on decisions for the AMS committee and for management. Keys: → next, ← back, 1–8 the outputs, F full screen, Esc opens the tool; a presentation clicker works. It runs fully offline with Odieman_Sample_Hospital.js beside the tool, and in a full copy it can also run on the data loaded now (your own, nothing saved) or on files dragged onto the stage.

Print antibiogram (A4) (5.7)

On the Antibiogram page: one page for the wards, built by the rules a laboratory would use. The first isolate per patient per organism (CLSI M39); organisms with fewer than 30 isolates greyed and not reported; intrinsic resistance printed as R* rather than counted (CLSI M100 Appendix B and the EUCAST expected resistant phenotypes); agents not reported for an organism or a specimen left blank, so nitrofurantoin appears for urine only; the AWaRe group on every column; the headline resistance figures (MRSA, third-generation-cephalosporin-resistant E. coli and Klebsiella, carbapenem resistance); the most used antibiotics next to their overall susceptibility; footnotes on the biases of a hospital antibiogram; signature lines for the laboratory and the AMS committee; and a pocket card to carry on the round.

Minimum Data and Check my data (5.3)

What AMS Pro needs for each analysis, file by file: the essential and recommended columns, the headings it recognises, and blank templates. Check my data reads the data loaded now and marks each analysis ready, limited, optional or not yet possible, with the reason and what to add. The reports carry the same check. The minimum data for AMS Pro.

Did It Work? in Stata (5.2)

Beside the figure and the monthly CSV: the months as a labelled Stata data file, and a Stata command file that re-runs exactly the model on the page (segmented regression with Newey–West standard errors, same lag) and stops if any estimate differs from AMS Pro's. Re-run outside Stata from the same files, the model matches AMS Pro to about 1 part in 1013; running the command file in Stata itself is still to come.

Manual entry on any form (5.5)

Fill in AMS Pro's own form, or load any REDCap project's data dictionary and fill in that form, with its branching logic. Send the entries to a plain CSV, a REDCap import file, a labelled Stata file, a WHONET-ready file where the form has culture results, or straight into AMS Pro's analysis.

Did It Work?

Pick the indicator, the ward and the month an intervention started (a new guideline, an antibiotic time-out, prescriber training, a restriction). AMS Pro compares the months before and after and fits an interrupted time series: it carries the trend that was already there forward and says whether the change after the start goes beyond it. It gives a 300-dpi figure, the monthly values as CSV, and methods and results text for a report or paper.

Intervention Impact, beside it

Kept for what Did It Work? does not do: patients admitted to a ward the dispensing export does not name, ward consumption tables, and results quarter by quarter. Each page links to the other.

A menu for each user

View · Change, at the top of the menu, asks who uses this computer (AMS team, clinician, IPC, laboratory and others) and shows the modules they need.

One drop for every file

Pharmacy, admissions, laboratory, procedures, the theatre list, culture results (WHONET or S/I/R sheets), store issues, completed ward-round forms and a project’s phase plan can be dropped together, in any order. AMS Pro reads the columns of each file and puts it in its place.

C/S Prophylaxis Project

A caesarean-section antibiotic prophylaxis project carried from baseline to the end-term report. Every C/S in the theatre list is linked to the woman’s admission and dispensing. Women already on antibiotics for an infection before surgery are set aside (n − a); each remaining woman gets a prophylaxis length (a single dose, one day, or longer) and, kept separate, a flag for a possible infection after surgery. Indicators by phase and by month, weekly or monthly interrupted time series (a run-in period can be left out), before–after tests, a file review that checks the labels against patient files (Cohen’s kappa), ward audits, and ward, committee, management and end-term reports in Word and PowerPoint. Open it in the demo ↗

AMS Ward Rounds

A one-page round form for any ward, printed from the dispensing export with every patient on antibiotics, the day of treatment, and what to look at first: no culture, resistance to the current drug, no change since the start (the 48–72 h review), injections that could go oral, long courses, antibiotics after an operation, duplicate cover. At the bedside the team ticks its findings and advice and whether the clinician agreed; the secretary types the form in afterwards. Each month the page shows findings per 10 patients reviewed, how much advice was agreed and carried out by the next round, how often each printed prompt was right, the teaching points, and the most common findings as topics for the next CME.
