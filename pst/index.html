The data is already collected. These tools turn it into stewardship.

Welcome — you probably scanned the poster. Hospital systems such as Afya eHMS already record almost everything an antimicrobial stewardship or pharmacovigilance team needs. What was missing was a tool to assemble and analyse it. Here are three — offline, free for Tanzania’s public health facilities, built by a hospital pharmacy technician.

The poster

Sub-theme: Digital Health and Innovation in Pharmacy

Offline Digital Tools for Point Prevalence Surveys, Antimicrobial Consumption Monitoring and Ward-Level ADR Detection at Tanga Regional Referral Hospital

Odindo Emmanuel Thomas1, Zalha Nuhu2

1 Matema Health Center, Kyela District Council, Mbeya, Tanzania
2 Tanga Regional Referral Hospital (Bombo), Tanga, Tanzania

Background. Hospital AMS teams in Tanzania are asked to do a lot with very little. They run point prevalence surveys, monitor antibiotic use and watch for adverse drug reactions, mostly on paper forms and spreadsheets that take days to clean and analyse. By the time a report reaches the committee, the data is often old. ADR reporting from the wards remains low, because busy ward staff do not see it as part of their daily work. We set out to build simple, affordable tools that let pharmacy staff collect, analyse and report their own data without internet access.

Methods. Drawing on three years of supporting the Tanga RRH AMS committee, we developed the Odieman suite, three linked offline tools. PPS Pro handles point prevalence surveys using WHO methodology. AMS Pro tracks antimicrobial consumption through DDD and DOT trends, Pareto analysis and cost estimates, and supports PDCA improvement cycles. VigiWard looks for ADR signals at ward level and analyses NCD and cardiometabolic patterns from routine pharmacy data. All three share one data source.

Results. The tools are in routine use by the AMS and pharmacovigilance teams at Tanga RRH. With PPS Pro, analysis is fully automated: once data collection ends, a complete PPS report is available with one click, while the findings are still relevant. Consumption and ADR data become committee-ready charts and reports the same way, on an ordinary computer without internet.

Conclusion. Practical digital tools can be built locally, by the people who do the work, at low cost. Timely PPS and consumption results allow early decisions on antibiotic use, while VigiWard brings pharmacovigilance closer to the wards, where most ADRs happen but few are reported. With support, these tools could reach other facilities and strengthen Tanzania’s National Action Plan on AMR and ADR reporting to TMDA.

The idea in 60 seconds

The gap

Stewardship and pharmacovigilance reports are compiled by hand, if at all — while the dispensing, lab and admission records that answer the questions sit unused in the hospital system.

The tools

AMS Pro for antibiotic use and resistance, PPS Pro for WHO point prevalence surveys, VigiWard for adverse reactions in the wards. One data source, each tool using what it needs.

In real use

Developed inside a regional referral hospital's AMS committee work, producing reports and slides from the hospital's own exports. Every figure shown publicly comes from simulated demonstration data.

Safe by design

Everything runs on the user's own computer. No server, no internet needed, no patient data transmitted — outputs are aggregate figures.
