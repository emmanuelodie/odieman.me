VigiWard

Pharmacovigilance signal detection from ordinary pharmacy and lab data — no dedicated ADR reporting system required to start finding patterns worth a chart review.

Download VigiWard See the SSA math

Why this exists

Formal adverse-drug-reaction reporting depends on someone noticing a reaction and filling out a form — and reporting rates in this setting run at roughly one report per million population per year, several orders of magnitude below what's actually happening. VigiWard works the other direction: it looks for prescribing patterns in data hospitals already generate — pharmacy dispensing sequences, lab result timing — that suggest a reaction occurred even when nobody filed a report.

What it does

Sequence symmetry analysis

For drug pairs where one commonly follows the other as a treatment for a side effect (the classic example: a laxative prescribed shortly after starting an opioid), the tool counts how often the sequence runs forward versus backward and flags pairs where that asymmetry is statistically unlikely to be chance — with a proper Wilson-derived confidence interval, not a raw ratio.

Lab-anchored signal detection

Cross-references pharmacy dispensing against lab result timing and mortality data to surface concerning temporal associations — always labeled as hypothesis-generating, never as a causal finding, since that determination needs clinical adjudication VigiWard doesn't replace.

TMDA-aligned draft reports

Flagged signals can be exported into a draft aligned with TMDA's yellow-form pharmacovigilance reporting structure — a head start for the person who still has to review the chart and make the clinical call, not a replacement for them.

Built-in caution

Every signal VigiWard surfaces carries the same three qualifiers, deliberately, every time:

QualifierWhat it means in practice
Crude, not adjustedNo correction yet for background prescribing-trend confounding
Minimum-power gatedA signal only fires with ≥ 5 discordant pairs and a lower CI bound above 1 — sparse data doesn't get to trigger a flag
Hypothesis-generatingPoints a reviewer at a chart worth pulling — it is not itself a diagnosis or a causality determination
